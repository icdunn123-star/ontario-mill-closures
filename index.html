Actual housing starts 150,000 annual pace benchmark
How to read this: “Actual construction” means housing starts—the number of homes where construction began, not homes completed. The dashed line is a simple 150,000-per-year benchmark (1.5 million divided evenly across 10 years); it is not the province's separate annual municipal housing-target measure. The 1.5-million goal is officially measured by the cumulative number of homes completed between 2022 and 2031, not housing starts. This chart uses conventional housing starts as an early construction indicator and proxy only. The “remaining” figure subtracts 2022–2025 starts from 1.5 million; the “behind pace” figure compares those starts with a straight-line 600,000 benchmark for the first four years. These are not the province's official completed-home progress totals. Ontario's separate housing-supply tracker also counts additional residential units and certain long-term-care, student-housing and retirement-home beds against municipal targets.
Sources: Ontario Ministry of Finance, 2026 Fall Statement, Economic Data Tables (Ontario housing starts, 2022–2025); Government of Ontario, Tracking housing supply progress.
Method: Closure year is defined here as the first year after a recorded active (Y) year in which the facility is marked inactive (N) in the workbook's TREES_Matrix. This is an analytical proxy, not an independently confirmed physical mill shutdown date. Locations are town/city-centre approximations based on the workbook's FacilityLocation field; markers are not exact mill-site coordinates. Facilities with no town/city value in the workbook are omitted from the map (8 records) but retained in the source workbook. Basemap tiles © Esri, USGS, NOAA.
